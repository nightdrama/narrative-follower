**Bear side:**
- The trigger is substantive, not a rumor: Anthropic's Amodei, OpenAI's Altman, and Musk (with Hassabis) publicly called for slowing frontier AI development pace over safety concerns on Sept 14 — a signal from the people setting AI capex, not speculation.

- High-beta, "priced for perfection" names across the AI-power and neocloud complex (Vistra, Talen, Oklo, NuScale, Bloom, CoreWeave, Nebius) were already de-rating into this event, suggesting the market was primed to treat any AI-slowdown headline as confirmation rather than noise.
**The crux:** Whether the next 1-2 weeks bring the same dip-buying/backlog-reaffirmation pattern seen in July and August, or upcoming hyperscaler commentary (Micron's Sept 30 print is a key test) actually shows AI capex decelerating — which would confirm this as a real inflection rather than a repeating scare.
**Chatter:** Elevated — the single most cross-cutting theme this week, touching nearly every section of the portfolio.

### 3. Custom ASIC share-shift (Broadcom/Marvell/Google TPU) vs. Nvidia's platform moat
**Names in play:** AVGO, MRVL, NVDA, TSM
**Bull side (ASIC):**
- Broadcom and Marvell together control roughly 95% of the AI ASIC co-design market; ASIC-based AI server shipments are projected to reach 27.8% of the market in 2026 (custom ASIC shipments +44.6% YoY vs. +16.1% for merchant GPUs) as Google, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, and OpenAI invest in purpose-built silicon.
- A fresh Marvell-Google TPU deal directly fueled "AVGO stock slides as MRVL-Google deal fuels competition fears" headlines — a live flashpoint pitting the two ASIC leaders' bull cases against each other and against Nvidia.
- Marvell projects up to $11B in AI ASIC revenue for 2026 (Amazon Trainium, Microsoft Maia); Broadcom carries a ~$73B AI backlog and a stated $230B AI-chip-revenue target by FY2028.
**Bear side (Nvidia moat / counter):**
- Nvidia is repositioning as a full AI infrastructure platform (NVLink/CUDA/systems), not just a GPU vendor — bulls-on-Nvidia argue platform lock-in blunts how much compute actually migrates to ASICs, and Jensen Huang has cited $1 trillion in orders for Blackwell/Vera Rubin through 2027.
- Counter-framing: "the half of the market that will never build its own chips" stays permanently captive to merchant GPUs regardless of ASIC share gains elsewhere; TSM's own record capex plans ($60-64B 2026, plus an added $100B Arizona commitment) reflect demand for both GPU and ASIC fabrication, not a zero-sum shift.
**The crux:** How much hyperscaler AI compute spend genuinely migrates from Nvidia GPUs to custom ASICs versus hyperscalers simply layering ASIC capacity on top of continued GPU buying.
**Chatter:** Elevated, sharpened this week by the Marvell-Google headline.

**The crux:** Whether GPU-backed debt and prepayment structures represent genuine backlog monetization or leveraged circularity that unwinds if AI capex actually slows (directly linked to Theme 1).

**Bear side:**
