### 1. Is the AI-capex boom a real backlog story or a circular-financing bubble about to pop?
**Names in play:** MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, META, ORCL, CRWV, NBIS, IREN, NVDA, MU, AMAT, PLTR, TSLA

The single loudest, most cross-cutting debate in the portfolio, and it's escalating fast — anchored by one increasingly vocal named bear.

**Bull side:**
- Nvidia's board authorized a fresh $150B buyback (total $235B, Sept 28) — Cramer called it trajectory-changing; bulls note ~30x trailing P/E is the "lowest valuation in 4 years" with a 58-buy/1-sell analyst split.
- Hyperscaler 2026 capex guide (~$725B combined across AMZN/GOOGL/META/MSFT) is framed by bulls as demand-driven: Oracle's $664B RPO, CoreWeave's ~$104B backlog (revenue >2x to $2.58B), and Meta's Muse AI assistant (2.8M downloads, stock +25% in September) are cited as proof the spend is converting to real revenue.
- Uncover Alpha (Substack) argues the "AI capex ROI is bad" thesis is "DEAD" post-earnings.

**Bear side:**
- Michael Burry (Scion) is the dominant named bear voice across the whole portfolio: Sept 28 comments call the AI bubble likely to "burst sooner than later," comparing the PHLX Semi Index's 92%-in-2-months run to the pre-2000 dot-com peak; he holds SOXX/SOXQ puts and disclosed shorts/positions touching NVDA, MU, AMAT, PLTR, TSLA, NBIS and CRWV (trimmed), calling AI hyperscalers/neoclouds "increasingly funding each other" — "shooting fish in a barrel."
- Oracle: S&P cut to BBB-, ~$58B of new debt in a year serving customers that are "concentrated and, in several cases, themselves loss-making" (leverage ~4.5x, near junk).

**The crux:** whether contracted backlog and usage growth are real enough to justify the debt, capex pace and circular financing arrangements funding the buildout — or whether a small, concentrated group of counterparties (Nvidia/OpenAI/Microsoft/Oracle/CoreWeave) is inflating demand that doesn't yet exist independently.

**Chatter:** very elevated and actively escalating — Burry is now the most-cited named voice across nearly every section of this portfolio.
