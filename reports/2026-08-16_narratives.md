## Top debates

### 1. Memory supercycle: structural AI re-rate vs. CXMT oversupply risk
**Names in play:** MU, SKHY, SNDK, WDC, STX (spillover: AAPL sourcing memory from Chinese suppliers amid tightness)
**Bull side:**
- AI has structurally shifted memory demand (HBM/DRAM/NAND as compute bottleneck), not another PC/phone-style cycle — cited repeatedly across AI earnings calls.
- Explosive short-term moves: SNDK +38%, SKHY +23%, STX +21%, WDC +17%, MU +13% in a week; YTD leaders SNDK +540%, SKHY +420%, MU +250%.
- Samsung memory profit guided +310% for 2026; memory names outperforming the $SMH semis index outright.
**Bear side:**
- History repeats — strong pricing invites supply. China's CXMT now ~7.7% of DRAM share, just raised $8.6B post-IPO (~$540B valuation) to fund fabs/R&D that will close the tech gap over time.
- Bear case rests on one metric: HBM mix must keep outgrowing bit growth, or conventional DRAM oversupply erodes pricing power.
**The crux:** does HBM/AI demand keep absorbing new bit supply faster than CXMT and Samsung/SK hynix add conventional capacity — or does 2026's pricing spike just pull forward the next glut?
**Chatter:** moderate, elevated vs. baseline — concentrated among semi/AI-infra traders, no single viral thread but consistent volume (views 1k–35k+ per post).

### 2. AI compute buildout: demand still outrunning supply vs. capex/order timing mismatch
**Names in play:** NVDA, AMD, TSM, MU (secondary); AMAT/LRCX/KLAC as the "beat but sold off" evidence
**Bull side:**
- Hyperscalers remain capacity-constrained today; an Anthropic roadshow memo circulating on X argues frontier models concentrate value rather than commoditize, and 12–24 month supply-chain lock-ins (pre-secured DC space, memory contracts) signal sticky, not speculative, demand.
- AMAT's earnings beat (record 8-quarter backlog visibility, advanced packaging as a >70% compounder, raised 2027 guidance) is framed as proof the AI-capex thesis is intact.
- Druckenmiller reportedly held TSM through the July memory rout — cited as a "tell" the cycle survives volatility.
**Bear side:**
- Timing mismatch: hyperscalers may have already committed capital for far more capacity than near-term demand absorbs, risking a pause in *new* GPU orders even without demand collapsing outright. NVDA's customer concentration (~39% from top accounts) amplifies the risk of any single PO change.
- AMAT sold off *despite* the beat — when the market sells "record quarters" across chipmakers, memory makers, and toolmakers simultaneously, that's read as valuation fatigue after a >200% YTD run, not a fundamentals problem.
**The crux:** is 2026 capex tracking real installed-base utilization, or is the industry building 2028 capacity against 2026 demand that hasn't shown up yet?
**Chatter:** low-to-moderate, thoughtful long-form takes rather than back-and-forth flame wars.

### 4. Neocloud: Jevons-paradox demand growth vs. bubble-repeat skepticism
**Names in play:** IREN, NBIS, CRWV (CORZ, GLXY, ORCL largely absent from real debate)
**Bull side:**
- Argument (from a high-engagement IREN CEO thread, 1,800+ likes/200k+ views): every new data center *increases* AI demand via Jevons paradox — cheaper compute creates new use cases faster than it satisfies existing ones. Supply (power, transmission, construction timelines) is the actual limiter, not demand.
- Hard numbers cited: IREN 4GW connected power + $12.5B contracts; NBIS +454% YoY revenue; CRWV $104B backlog.
**Bear side:**
- Recurring "is AI spend too high" skepticism — past tech buildouts eventually saw supply catch up or demand disappoint; some question whether incremental AI demand actually accrues to these listed players.
- Execution matters more than scarcity talk: IREN has been "dead money" since November despite the power-capacity story, trailing NBIS (+87%) and CRWV (+73%) — the market is rewarding delivered contracts over potential.
**The crux:** backlog-to-FCF conversion — can these names turn contracted capacity into real free cash flow before capex/financing costs catch up, or is the multiple pricing in monetization that hasn't happened yet?
**Chatter:** moderate-to-high, the most substantive multi-post debate in the whole portfolio this cycle.
