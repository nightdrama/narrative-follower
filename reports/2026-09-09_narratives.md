**Bear side:**

**The crux:** contracted, cash-flow-generating IPPs (CEG/VST/NRG/TLN) are being sold off almost as hard as pre-revenue SMR bets (OKLO/SMR) on the same merchant-power-curve doubts — the market hasn't yet decided whether "earnings-backed" actually insulates a name from the broader AI-power sentiment swings.

### 3. Memory: structural HBM/NAND supercycle vs. recurring glut-fear whiplash
**Names in play:** MU, SNDK, WDC, STX, SKHY, AMAT, LRCX, KLAC, ASML
**Bull side:**
- BofA calls 2026 a memory "supercycle similar to the 1990s boom" (DRAM revenue +51% YoY, NAND +45%); Morgan Stanley models DRAM +62%/NAND +75% pricing in 2026. Samsung/SK hynix raised HBM3E prices ~20% for 2026 orders. DRAM spot prices are reportedly up ~700% over the past year, feeding directly into semicap: Applied Materials raised its CY2026 equipment-growth outlook from "over 20%" to "more than 30%," and BofA/Cantor hiked targets across KLAC/LRCX/AMAT/ASML.
- SK hynix's chairman warned global memory supply could stay ~20% below demand through 2030; Goldman flagged (Sept 8) improving technical setups in MU/SNDK, calling AI-trade positioning "relatively light" after a consolidation.
**Bear side:**
- The group has whipsawed on glut fears repeatedly: SanDisk -11%, Seagate -7%, Micron -4% one session; WDC -16%, SNDK -11%, MU -6% another — as Samsung/SK hynix supply additions and a possible AI-capex plateau feed "smart money is rotating out of the AI trade" framing.
- A semicap-specific bear point: multi-year long-term agreements now lock in pricing/allocation years in advance, meaning the eventual cyclical downturn (when memory oversupply does arrive) will show up later and be harder to time than in past cycles — the risk is deferred, not resolved.
- China's "50% Rule" (domestic-equipment purchase mandates) is pressuring AMAT/KLA/LRCX's China revenue independent of the memory cycle itself.
**The crux:** whether current HBM/NAND pricing power is a durable, multi-year structural shortage that justifies both the memory names' re-rating and the semicap capex hikes riding on it — or a cyclical spike that new Korean/Chinese capacity erodes on a longer lag than the market is pricing, given each glut scare has so far reversed within weeks.
**Chatter:** elevated, with sizable single-day swings on both sides. (SIMO, RMBS, CAMT, ONTO, ENTG saw no distinct coverage this pass.)

### 4. AI compute silicon and optics: who keeps pricing power as the buildout compounds

**Bull side:**

Broadcom guides toward ~$56B in AI semiconductor revenue this fiscal year; TSMC reported a 90% jump in equipment demand and raised 2026 capex guidance to $60-64B while still saying it can't keep pace.
- Optics names have posted outsized YTD moves (Lumentum +115%, Credo +77%, AAOI +111%, Coherent +25%, Astera Labs +59% per one tally) on real hyperscaler bookings, not just narrative.
**Bear side:**
- The same earnings cycle split the group hard: Cisco fell ~4.4% after-hours despite the beat (margin-guidance concern), and Coherent dropped ~16% on soft Q1 guidance even after beating — while Nokia and Ciena rose on the same days. "Optics stocks divide" is now a recognized, named pattern, not a one-off.
- Astera Labs fell ~12% in a single session on valuation-reset concerns (19x forward P/S vs. Credo's 16x) tied explicitly to hyperscaler-capex-slowdown risk; a structural long-term threat is also flagged — hyperscalers designing their own switch/optical silicon (Cisco Silicon One, potential co-packaged-optics shifts) could erode Broadcom/Arista's merchant-silicon position over a multi-year horizon.
- Cross-cutting bubble concern: NVDA/AVGO price-to-sales above 30x, "circular deal" financing (Microsoft-OpenAI-Azure, Oracle-OpenAI-Nvidia loops) flagged by Bloomberg's own graphic tracker as inflating reported demand; a depreciation-accounting critique estimates hyperscalers are understating true AI-hardware costs by ~$176B across 2026-2028 by using 5-6 year useful lives instead of an estimated 2-3 year economic life.
**The crux:** the AI buildout itself is not in question, but the market is now pricing single-quarter guidance nuances (not demand) as the swing factor for these stocks, even as the deeper circular-financing and depreciation-accounting bear cases — which nobody has disproven, just argued around — sit underneath the whole group.
**Chatter:** elevated, concentrated on the Cisco/Coherent earnings divergence and the broader AI-bubble discourse. (GFS, CBRS, SITM, KEYS, TSEM, AXTI, APH, TEL, CLS, TER saw no distinct coverage this pass.)
