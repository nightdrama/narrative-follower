### 1. The AI bubble fight: Michael Burry's short campaign vs. record backlogs
**Names in play:** MU, INTC, NVDA, ORCL, NBIS, PLTR, META, CRWV, SOXX-adjacent names broadly
**Bull side:**
- Every named company just posted record or near-record numbers: NVDA data center revenue +117% YoY with supply commitments to its own suppliers doubling to $279B; Oracle RPO $638B (+363% YoY); Palantir +93% YoY revenue with 157% net dollar retention.
- Bulls frame Burry's public shorts (MU, INTC-adjacent, Oracle, Micron, Nebius, Palantir puts) as a known-perma-bear calling top on a structurally different, supply-constrained cycle — not the 2000 vintage.
- Jensen Huang: "we have supply for 70% growth… demand is much higher than that."
**Bear side:**
- Burry's core argument: hyperscalers depreciate GPUs over 5-6 years vs. a real 2-3 year economic life, allegedly overstating 2026-2028 industry profits by ~$176B (Oracle ~27%, Meta ~21% overstated).
- A widely-cited MIT report found 95% of enterprises investing in generative AI have seen no ROI yet.
- MU trades further above its 200-day moving average than at any point since 1984 (including the dot-com peak), per Burry's own framing.
**The crux:** whether any hyperscaler quietly revises GPU depreciation schedules downward, or a forced write-down/impairment shows up — that would validate the bear case in one data point.
**Chatter:** elevated and rising — Burry is now the single most-cited name across memory, CPU, neocloud, and mega-cap sections simultaneously.

### 2. Circular financing: is neocloud/AI demand real or vendor-funded?
**Names in play:** ORCL, CRWV, NBIS, AMD, NVDA, MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, META
**Bull side:**
- CoreWeave backlog $104.2B backed by 4.2GW of contracted power; Nebius revenue +454% YoY with a fresh multibillion-dollar Nvidia equity stake; OpenAI's $1.1T in named commitments spans Azure/Oracle/AWS/Nvidia/CoreWeave, locking in the supply chain.
- AMD's Anthropic (2GW MI450) and OpenAI deals prompted Melius to raise 2027 EPS estimates 35%; management says Helios demand is "tracking ahead of forecast."
**Bear side:**
- ~$300B of Oracle's RPO reportedly traces to OpenAI alone, which runs at ~$40B annualized revenue and a ~$14B/yr loss — the classic Nvidia→OpenAI→Oracle→Nvidia loop critics call circular.
- AMD's OpenAI + Meta warrants cover up to ~20% of AMD's shares at a $0.01 strike — Morgan Stanley says this makes "the profit story much uglier" than headline deal size suggests.
- CoreWeave's own backlog math: at ~7-9% operating margin, $104B of backlog nets ~$8B of lifetime operating income against $35B of debt.
**The crux:** whether actual compute utilization/consumption data from anchor tenants (OpenAI, Anthropic) tracks the headline contract dollars in coming quarters, or the first contract deferral/renegotiation surfaces.
**Chatter:** elevated — Jim Chanos has publicly echoed Burry on CoreWeave's balance sheet; one VC voice is calling for a credit-driven neocloud shakeout between Oct 2026 and Mar 2027.

### 2b. Memory supercycle: sold-out HBM/DRAM/NAND/HDD pricing vs. cyclical-top risk
**Names in play:** MU, SKHY, SNDK, WDC, STX, AVGO (HBM demand side)
**Bull side:**
- Micron's 2026 HBM capacity is sold out, TrendForce sees 2027 HBM contract prices up 70-140%; SK hynix Q2 revenue +51% QoQ with a 76% operating margin; SanDisk floor NAND pricing near $0.29/GB.
- WDC nearline HDD exabytes +37% YoY, sold out through 2026 and negotiating supply deals into 2029-31; Morgan Stanley sees HDD supply running 10-15% short of demand into 2028.
**Bear side:**
- Michael Burry disclosed a Micron short, citing 34 drawdowns >30% in the last 42 years and MU trading further above its 200-day MA than at any point since 1984.
- A WSJ report on "hidden tech commitments" (unrelated to memory fundamentals) triggered a one-day selloff — SNDK -9%, MU -7%, WDC -5% — evidence the rally is partly sentiment/momentum, not pure fundamentals.
- A new DRAM price-fixing class action against Samsung/SK hynix/Micron alleges ~697% cumulative price inflation, adding legal/regulatory tail risk to the very pricing power bulls cite.
**The crux:** whether Q3/Q4 2026 DRAM/NAND contract pricing actually prints in line with guidance (TrendForce forecasts +13-18% DRAM QoQ) — a miss hands the win to Burry's cyclical-top call.
**Chatter:** elevated, whipsawing week to week (Musk/Diamandis "memory is the rate-limiter of the Agentic Era" exchange sparked one rally leg; rate-fear headlines have twice knocked the group down 5-7% in a session with no company-specific news).

### 3. Custom silicon reshuffling: Broadcom's moat vs. Marvell's Google win, and vendor-financed CPU deals

**Bull side:**

**Bear side:**

- Intel stock traded below its own $30B raise price during the week; bears note foundry still has no committed marquee external 18A customer.
**The crux:** how much of Google's incremental TPU/inference-chip volume actually shifts to Marvell vs. staying with Broadcom, visible in the next 1-2 quarters of disclosed custom-silicon revenue.
**Chatter:** elevated (AVGO -5% same-day on the Marvell/Google news; Marvell reports its own Q2 FY27 print just after this window).

Abbott, pending an ERCOT/PUCT audit of ~474GW of requests due Dec 10) — a direct overhang on CORZ/GLXY/IREN buildouts.

### 6. China tech war: DUV lithography breakthrough and tightening export controls
**Names in play:** ASML, LRCX, AMAT, KLAC, ENTG, NVDA, INTC
**Bull side:**
- ASML's Q2 was a record booking quarter (€13.2B backlog quarter), FY guidance raised to €43-45B — cited by bulls as vindication that Chinese domestic lithography (reportedly ~5 immersion DUV units in 2026, ~20 in 2027, targeting mature 28nm) is "a decade behind, not a threat to EUV."
**Bear side:**
- The China DUV report still knocked ASML down 4.6-7% intraday with AMAT/LRCX/KLAC falling in sympathy; Lam Research is now separately restricted from shipping to Hua Hong, China's second-largest chipmaker — a direct revenue hit, not just sentiment.
- Nvidia again sold zero H20 chips to China in Q2 and is guiding zero again for Q3 (only $2-5B of potential upside "if geopolitical issues resolve").
**The crux:** how fast Chinese domestic lithography players scale past 28nm toward 14nm-and-below volume — the "~20 units in 2027" guide either holds (bull) or accelerates (bear).
**Chatter:** elevated — chip-equipment names were front-page material on this headline, part of a broader >$1T market-cap chip-sector drawdown in late July/August.

### 7. Optics: co-packaged optics threat vs. pluggable-transceiver durability, and hyperscaler concentration risk
**Names in play:** COHR, LITE, AAOI, FN, CRDO, ALAB, ANET, CIEN, GLW
**Bull side:**
- Nvidia put $4B directly into Coherent and Lumentum ($2B each) for co-packaged-optics scale-out; Ciena's $7.7B backlog and book-to-bill at highs give visibility into 2027; CPO shipments are projected to reach only ~35% penetration by 2030, meaning pluggable transceiver volumes keep rising (50M→200M units) even as CPO grows.
**Bear side:**
- Valuations are stretched across the group (Credo trailing P/E ~103); Astera Labs, Credo, and Ciena all carry heavy hyperscaler concentration (Credo's top 4 customers = 87% of revenue), so any single hyperscaler capex guide-down would hit disproportionately.
- Anthropic's $65B ARR print missing an $80B+ whisper number triggered an optics-specific risk-off wave (LITE -10%, AXTI -12%, GLW -8%) even though chip names held up better — a sign this complex trades on its own sentiment, not just the broader AI trade.
**The crux:** Nvidia's actual CPO attach rate once Spectrum-X Photonics ships in 2H26 — a low attach rate favors the pluggable-makers near-term.
**Chatter:** elevated — the single most actively-traded/discussed section, with near-daily double-digit swings across the whole optics complex on sympathy moves.
