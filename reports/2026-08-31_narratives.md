### 1. AI capex: real, monetizing buildout vs. circular-financing bubble
**Names in play:** NVDA, MSFT, META, GOOGL, AMZN, ORCL, CRWV, NBIS, AMD, MU, PLTR, SOXX broadly
**Bull side:**
- Nvidia's Aug 26/27 print: Q2 revenue $96.2B (+106% YoY, beat $92.2B consensus), data-center revenue $89.0B (+117% YoY), Q3 guide $108B vs. $104.2B consensus; Jensen Huang guided ~70% FY28 revenue growth. CFO guided top-five hyperscaler capex to $1.3T over the next year, up from $800B. Stock +7-8.7%, adding ~$442B market cap, the biggest single-day surge since April 2025 — and it rippled through the whole complex (Marvell +5.8%, Micron +4.5%, Salesforce +11%).
- Microsoft: Azure crossed $100B annualized revenue, +43% YoY (beat 40% est.), capex guidance held flat — read as proof spend is monetizing (+16% on the print). Amazon: AWS +37% YoY (fastest since 2021), $169B annualized run-rate, backlog $496B. CoreWeave revenue +112% YoY, backlog $104-125B; Oracle RPO $638B.
**Bear side:**
- Michael Burry has escalated his public circular-financing critique across multiple names at once: he calls Nvidia's OpenAI guarantee a "$105B circular financing scheme," the broader $500B AI financing web "byzantine" with "shades of Enron," and says he's "maybe even more aggressive" than in 2020 shorting Nvidia, Oracle, Nebius, Micron, and SOXX broadly (short book >21% of his portfolio, stock only). He calls CoreWeave "an off-balance-sheet SPV designed to lose money" but won't short it directly, citing its "meme vibe."
- Meta narrowed 2026 capex to $130-145B (~2x 2025) with $10.8B escrowed for infrastructure not releasing until 2028-2030; most analysts now expect Meta FCF negative through 2027. Oracle burned $23.7B of FY26 FCF against $55.7B capex, guiding capex to $90-95B for FY27 funded partly by ~$40B new debt; over half its $638B backlog ties to OpenAI, whose IPO just slipped to 2027. CoreWeave carries $35.6B debt, 8.94x debt-to-equity, 10.75x net debt/EBITDA.
- Macro overlay: Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Aug 29 Jackson Hole speech pushed back on AI-productivity justifying current inflation, sending 2-year yields to ~4.35% and cooling the post-Nvidia rally into month-end — a fresh discount-rate risk layered on every long-duration AI capex story here.
**The crux:** whether hyperscaler/neocloud spending is now cleanly separating into monetizing capex (Microsoft, Amazon, Nvidia's own beat-and-raise) versus debt- and vendor-financing-fueled builders (Meta, Oracle, CoreWeave, Nebius) — and whether Burry's circular-financing thesis is a real structural crack or noise around an accelerating cycle.
**Chatter:** elevated — the single largest, most persistent theme across every section, amplified by Nvidia's earnings and now Burry's expanding short list.

### 2. Memory/HBM pricing supercycle — durable structural undersupply, or a Burry-flagged, Apple-threatened top?
**Names in play:** MU, WDC, SNDK, STX, SKHY, SIMO; spills into NVDA's own margin guide and CAMT/ONTO (HBM packaging)
**Bull side:**
- Micron's HBM is sold out through 2026 on ~$100B of binding multi-year contracts; SanDisk signed five long-term deals worth $94B minimum revenue / $91B RPO, CEO says "over four years of demand visibility." Gartner pegs 2026 DRAM price growth at ~125%, NAND at ~234%. SK hynix's operating margin hit 76% on 58% HBM share. Seagate: FY26 revenue +34%, record $3.1B FCF, 22 of 23 analysts Buy.
**Bear side:**
- Michael Burry disclosed a fresh Micron short (added near $924 after an initial $1,052 entry), citing 34 drawdowns >30% over 42 years and a median ROIC of just 4% ("frankly terrible"). Reports Aug 24 that the Trump administration may let Apple source DRAM/NAND from China's CXMT/YMTC (pending the Sept 24 Trump-Xi summit) sent SanDisk -9%, Micron and WDC -7% each same day — even though both suppliers remain on the Pentagon's Section 1260H list, a complication bulls cite. WDC and SNDK both beat Q4 estimates yet fell double digits on the print (WDC -10.8% after-hours) on guidance that merely met consensus after huge YTD runs (WDC +202%, SNDK +469% into the print). Nvidia itself flagged rising memory costs compressing its own gross-margin guide (71-72% vs. 75%) — memory inflation now biting the very companies it's supposed to benefit.
**The crux:** whether hyperscaler HBM/DRAM commitments hold durably, or whether a geopolitical wildcard (Chinese memory sourcing for Apple) plus Burry-style cyclicality skepticism unwinds pricing faster than the "sold out through 2027" consensus assumes.
**Chatter:** very high — the most argued-about corner of the market, with named-short-driven volatility (multiple >5% single-day swings across the whole group).

### 4. Custom silicon ASIC wars: has Google's Marvell deal cracked Broadcom's moat?
**Names in play:** AVGO, MRVL, NVDA
**Bull side (Marvell):** Google expanded its custom-chip deal with Marvell (Aug 19) covering TPU-ecosystem products, reportedly worth up to $120B over 6.5 years, plus a warrant for ~59M MRVL shares; Marvell's own Q2 print was a record beat (revenue +37% YoY, data-center revenue +46% YoY).
**Bear side:** Broadcom shares dipped on the news, read as dilution of its decade-long TPU co-design relationship with Google; TrendForce reported Marvell and AMD "reportedly shake up Google TPU race, putting Broadcom, MediaTek under pressure." Marvell itself sold off on its own beat-and-raise print ("Why Marvell Stock Is Falling After A Beat-And-Raise Quarter") on custom-silicon margin-dilution concerns even as the segment is set to more than double by FY28.
**The crux:** whether Google's expanded Marvell relationship is a genuine, repeatable diversification away from Broadcom's dominant custom-silicon position, or normal multi-sourcing behavior from a hyperscaler at Google's scale that both suppliers still split between them (~95% of the ASIC co-design market combined) — Broadcom's early-September earnings is the next real test.
**Chatter:** high — the most detailed named ticker-vs-ticker rivalry story found this window.

**Bear side:**

**The crux:** whether the optics complex's huge YTD run (many names +60-225%) reflects durable content-per-rack gains from AI racks, or whether it's now the most crowded, highest-beta corner of the trade — most vulnerable to the Theme 3 "beat but sell off" pattern on the next print.
**Chatter:** elevated — clearly the more actively (and directly X-sourced) discussed of the two power/optics sections this window.

**Chatter:** normal, with periodic elevated spikes on specific export-control headlines and earnings.

### 8. AI-native software: real monetization vs. customer concentration and stretched multiples
**Names in play:** MDB, SNOW, DDOG, NET, AKAM, CRCL, FSLY, DOCN
**Bull side:** MongoDB's FCF jumped from $121M to $500M in a year (a real "operating leverage inflection" argument), and it trades at 62x forward earnings vs. Snowflake's 156x — a relative-value bull case heading into their Sept 1-2 prints. Cloudflare beat and raised (+35.9% revenue), drawing PT hikes to $400 (Citi); Akamai's $1.8B, 7-year Anthropic cloud-infrastructure deal drove its best week since 2013 (+42%); DigitalOcean rose on an AI-agents launch with RPO up 12x YoY.
**Bear side:** MongoDB's core Atlas growth is guided to decelerate to 21-23% from 29%, and UBS found "limited evidence of material AI pull-through" in the numbers yet. Snowflake faces a stark private-market comp problem: Databricks just crossed a $7B run-rate at >80% growth, raised at a $190B valuation (1.6x Snowflake's public market cap on comparable revenue). Datadog explicitly guided to a spending slowdown from its largest AI-native customer (OpenAI) while trading near 664x P/E. Circle got hit with a sharp Morgan Stanley downgrade (PT cut from $106 to $38) as a 140+-company "Open USD" stablecoin consortium (Stripe, Coinbase, Visa, Mastercard, BlackRock) directly attacks its reserve-income economics — shares fell >17% on the launch news.
**The crux:** whether AI-native workloads are becoming a durable, diversified revenue layer for these platforms (MDB/SNOW's Sept 1-2 prints are the next test), or a concentrated dependency on a handful of frontier-lab customers and competitors whose own spending pace is itself tangled up in Theme 1's capex debate.
**Chatter:** elevated, building sharply into the MDB/SNOW earnings cluster; Circle's stablecoin-competition story is the sharpest idiosyncratic bear thread in the group.
