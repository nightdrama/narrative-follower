### Memory supercycle: structural rerating or a peak-cycle blowoff?
**Names in play:** MU, WDC, SNDK, STX, SIMO, RMBS, SKHY
**Bull side:**
- DRAM contract prices up 58–63% QoQ and NAND up 70–75% in Q2 as Samsung/SK Hynix/Micron reallocate capacity to HBM, starving consumer DRAM/NAND supply — the "worst shortage in 15 years."
- Micron net profit reportedly up ~770% YoY; SK Hynix operating margin near 72%. Stocktwits sentiment ran "extremely bullish" on WDC/MU/SNDK earlier in the week, with message volume spiking.
- Bulls frame this as durable — HBM reallocation persists into 2027+, so the squeeze on legacy memory doesn't quickly resolve.
**Bear side:**
- WDC shed over 20% this week even as MU/SNDK held up — traders debating whether the WDC/SanDisk earnings reaction marks the top.
- A price-collusion lawsuit against memory makers is weighing on sentiment; retail chatter split between "bullish" and "bearish" tags on the same names within days.
- Skeptics note the "red-hot memory rally" has repeatedly looked stretched (Samsung strike headlines, Iran-related swings) — a sign the move is trading on momentum, not just fundamentals.
**The crux:** whether the HBM-driven supply reallocation is a multi-year structural repricing of memory (bulls) or a spot-price spike that mean-reverts once fabs catch up / demand normalizes (bears) — WDC's post-earnings drop is the test case everyone is watching.
**Chatter:** elevated, with sharp swings day to day.

### AI capex/bubble: real earnings power or late-cycle euphoria?
**Names in play:** NVDA, PLTR, MSFT, GOOGL, META, AMZN, AAPL, TSLA
**Bull side:**
- Mention-tracking shows heavily lopsided bull/bear ratios (e.g., NVDA ~67 bull vs 9 bear mentions, AMZN 21 bull/0 bear) — social sentiment still skews strongly optimistic on the mega-cap AI complex.
- Bulls argue this isn't a "is AI real" debate anymore — earnings and capex are already showing up in results, not just promises.
- PLTR bulls call it one of the decade's most important AI companies, pointing to accelerating government/enterprise adoption.
**Bear side:**
- Insiders at NVDA, PLTR, GOOGL and META have been steady net sellers over the trailing year — read by bears as a tell on valuation.
- Debate has shifted from "is AI real" to "timing" — how long spending can outrun doubts before a violent repricing.
- PLTR bears see hype-driven multiples with little room for disappointment; concentration risk in a handful of names is repeatedly flagged.
**The crux:** not whether AI demand is real, but whether current valuations already price in years of flawless execution — insider selling vs. bullish retail/analyst mention ratios is the tension point.
**Chatter:** elevated, arguably the single loudest cross-portfolio debate.

### Neocloud: compute-shortage play or capacity glut waiting to happen?
**Names in play:** CRWV, NBIS, IREN, CORZ, ORCL
**Bull side:**
- Framed as the "purest public-market bet" on the AI compute shortage; Morgan Stanley-cited estimates of $1.2T (2027) and $1.4T (2028) AI/HPC capex support the growth story.
- IREN's 5-year, $3.4B Nvidia AI cloud contract plus up to $2.1B Nvidia investment (toward 600k GPUs) cited as proof hyperscaler-grade demand is real.
- A Stocktwits poll reportedly found 65% of retail respondents buying the dip.
**Bear side:**
- Meta's move to rent out excess AI compute capacity to enterprises is fueling fears that hyperscalers themselves could undercut independent neocloud providers.
- IREN down ~37% over the past month despite the Nvidia contract — bears say the deal is already priced in and financing/dilution risk dominates.
- A widely quoted comment from a "Palo Alto CEO" warning a neocloud price crash is coming has become a bear talking point; CRWV/NBIS retail sentiment logged as "bearish" on low volume even as IREN ran hot.
**The crux:** whether hyperscaler in-house/excess capacity (Meta renting out compute) structurally caps neocloud pricing power, or whether GPU/power scarcity keeps neoclouds essential regardless.
**Chatter:** elevated but choppy — sharp reversals within the same week.

### Nuclear/power: proven earners vs. pre-revenue SMR speculation
**Names in play:** CEG, VST, TLN, OKLO, SMR, NNE, CCJ
**Bull side:**
- Hyperscaler PPAs are real and large — Meta's 20-year, 2,600+ MW deal with Vistra and its Oklo funding/power-purchase arrangement are cited as proof AI demand is translating into signed nuclear contracts.
- AI data center power demand projected to roughly quadruple by 2034, framed as a multi-decade tailwind for the whole nuclear complex, including uranium supplier CCJ.
**Bear side:**
- OKLO, SMR and NNE are down 57–78% from their October peaks — bears note these are pre-revenue/loss-making developers with first commercial power years away, dependent on subsidies and federal contracts.
- Only the deregulated operators (VST, CEG, TLN) currently generate real nuclear earnings; the SMR cohort is trading almost entirely on narrative.
**The crux:** the market has already bifurcated the group — cash-generating operators with signed PPAs (CEG/VST/TLN) vs. speculative pre-revenue SMR developers (OKLO/SMR/NNE) — and the debate is whether the SMR names' steep drawdowns are a healthy repricing of execution risk or a buying opportunity into 2027+ delivery.
**Chatter:** elevated on the SMR names specifically; CEG/VST/TLN comparatively quieter.

### Data center infrastructure: decade-long buildout vs. political/financing pushback
**Names in play:** VRT, ETN, PWR, MTZ
**Bull side:**
- Sell-side conviction is unusually one-sided — Vertiv cited at 86% bullish / 0% bearish analyst sentiment, with commentary that the buildout "hasn't even taken the field yet."
- Physical build-out constraints (power, cooling) mean near-term supply can't catch demand, supporting pricing power for VRT/ETN-type suppliers.
**Bear side:**

- Retail sentiment has soured on some infrastructure names (one composite reading cited as "bearish" at 34.59) amid questions about how hyperscaler capex commitments (e.g., OpenAI's reported $1.4T in commitments) actually get financed.
**The crux:** whether physical/political constraints on new data center buildout become a real bottleneck to capex realization, or remain noise against analysts' near-unanimous bullish view.
**Chatter:** normal, skewed more bullish among analysts than among retail.
