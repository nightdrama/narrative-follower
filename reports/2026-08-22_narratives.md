### 1. Is AI capex converting to revenue, or is the chain getting ahead of itself?
**Names in play:** NVDA, AVGO, MRVL, TSM, CBRS, AMAT, LRCX, KLAC, ASML, VRT, GEV, CRWV, ORCL, NBIS, GOOGL, AMZN, MSFT, META
**Bull side:**
- Hard revenue confirmation keeps arriving: Google Cloud +82% YoY, AWS +37% (fastest since 2021), Azure +43%; TSM raised 2026 capex to $60–64B on tight leading-edge capacity; GE Vernova's data-center order backlog hit $176B (book-to-bill ~2.2x).
- Lam Research's CEO describes "unprecedented demand" with order visibility stretching into 2027+; wafer-fab-equipment order momentum hasn't broken despite the scares below.
**Bear side:**
- Anthropic's disclosed $65B run-rate missed the ~$80B whisper number and a WSJ report tallying ~$3T of off-balance-sheet AI commitments across nine Big Tech names triggered a broad Aug 18–20 de-risking that hit semi cap (-4 to -5%), optics, nuclear/power, and neocloud simultaneously.
- Vertiv missed Q2 sales ($3.27B vs $3.38B est.), fell ~17%, and now faces four separate law-firm fraud investigations into whether backlog/deployment timelines were overstated — a credibility hit for the whole "record backlog" bull case across DC infrastructure names.
- Michael Burry keeps recirculating a 2008/1999-2000-style bubble comparison specifically targeting circular AI financing.
**The crux:** whether backlog and cloud-revenue growth keep converting into recognized cash flow at the pace priced in, or whether Vertiv/Anthropic-style credibility misses become the pattern that reprices the entire AI-capex supply chain.
**Chatter:** elevated — the dominant cross-sector story of the window, resurfacing in nearly every section.

### 2. Neocloud: is debt-funded backlog growth real demand or a financing house of cards?
**Names in play:** CRWV, ORCL, NBIS, IREN, CORZ
**Bull side:**
- CoreWeave's backlog exceeds $104B (+$25B fresh Q3 commitments), Nvidia raised its stake to $2B; Nebius revenue +454% YoY with 2026 power target raised to 5GW; Oracle RPO ballooned to $638B (+363%).
- Bulls argue the Nvidia-invests-in-its-customers loop simply reflects genuine, contracted compute scarcity, not manufactured demand.
**Bear side:**
- CoreWeave's debt load (~$30–35B), negative FCF (-$7.25B), and a current ratio of 0.46 drove Martin Shkreli to publicly disclose a short on X (a ~2M-view thread) — he covered without explanation about a week later. Oracle carries $124.7B non-current debt and -$23.7B FCF, flagged as "most vulnerable hyperscaler" alongside Amazon.
- Nebius fell 10% on an upsized $5B convertible-note pricing (dilution concern); the Bank for International Settlements' 2026 Annual Report named circular AI financing one of three top global financial-stability risks — elevating the debate from chatter to institutional concern.
**The crux:** is the RPO/backlog growth genuine forward cash flow, or is it being purchased with debt/dilution the companies can't service if AI demand growth decelerates even modestly?
**Chatter:** high — the Shkreli CRWV thread was the single most-discussed individual episode across all 13 sections.

### 3. Memory: structural HBM/NAND supercycle vs. classic cycle-top unwind
**Names in play:** MU, SNDK, WDC, STX, SKHY, SIMO, RMBS
**Bull side:**
- HBM reportedly sold out through 2026 on multi-year take-or-pay contracts (16 signed by Micron); Samsung itself warned the shortage worsens into 2027–28; Seagate's HAMR ramp now covers 40% of nearline exabytes with 13 straight quarters of margin expansion; post-selloff valuations look statistically cheap (MU ~4.8x forward P/E).
**Bear side:**
- A WSJ report on Aug 18 triggered a sharp rout (SNDK -9%, MU -7%, WDC -5%); Micron, Samsung, SK hynix and the Roundhill Memory ETF are all down >20% from highs — technically a bear market. Strategists quoted warning that record margins historically invite new supply, which would erode pricing.
**The crux:** is this a buyable pullback inside a contract-backed multi-year supercycle, or the opening move of the standard memory-cycle unwind as elevated margins draw in competing capacity?
**Chatter:** high — explicit sentiment-score whipsaws reported (MU sentiment read 35, then rebounded to 78, within one week), the most volatile single sector of the window.

### 4. Custom ASIC vs. merchant GPU: is Broadcom's moat cracking?
**Names in play:** MRVL, AVGO, GOOGL, NVDA
**Bull side (Marvell/custom-silicon camp):** Google granted Marvell warrants worth up to $12.2B as part of an expanded TPU-ecosystem deal (inference accelerators, NICs, storage/memory controllers) worth up to ~$120B cumulative revenue through 2033; Stocktwits retail sentiment flipped "extremely bullish" with message volume up ~140%.
**Bear side (Broadcom/skeptics):** AVGO fell ~5% on the news (~$87B market cap erased) on fears Google is diversifying away from single-vendor exclusivity; the "win" for Marvell itself carries real dilution (59M warrant shares at $206.58 strike), which triggered a follow-on 6% MRVL pullback once investors did the math.
**The crux:** does expanding custom-ASIC demand grow the pie for all merchant/custom silicon players, or does each new multi-sourcing deal fragment the pricing power that built Broadcom's premium multiple?
**Chatter:** high — one of the most-discussed single stories in the chips & compute section.

**Chatter:** high — multiple consecutive "nuclear stocks plunge" articles Aug 18–20, dedicated Stocktwits threads on OKLO/SMR/NNE.

### 6. Optics/networking: China-transceiver-ban tailwind vs. "priced for perfection" selloff
**Names in play:** LITE, COHR, AAOI, FN, CRDO, ALAB, GLW, CIEN
**Bull side:**
- A proposed FCC ban on Chinese optical transceivers would strip ~60% of China's supply (56% global share) from US hyperscalers, forcing share to Western vendors — LITE, COHR and AAOI all popped on the news; real demand keeps confirming (COHR Datacenter/Comms +59%, ALAB +104% YoY, CRDO +157% YoY).
**Bear side:**
- Valuations are "priced for perfection" (LITE ~26x revenue, COHR ~127x TTM P/E); an Aug 20 broad sell-off hit AAOI (-17%), COHR/LITE (-7 to -12%), and FN (-20%+ post-earnings guidance deceleration); Michael Burry's Aug 21 circular-financing post amplified the anxiety; a separate "CPO rollout delay" report (Nvidia's GB300 still leans copper-heavy) undercut the optical-inevitability timeline.
**The crux:** is "good but decelerating" growth enough to justify current multiples, or is this the top of the AI-optics momentum trade?
**Chatter:** high — the Aug 20 selloff plus Burry's Aug 21 post was the heaviest single catalyst in the group this window.

### 7. Power semi & industrials: AI-datacenter tailwind vs. broader auto/industrial correction

**Bull side:**

**Bear side:**

**The crux:** is AI-driven power-management demand broad and durable enough to offset lingering auto/industrial destocking, or is negative-margin AI revenue (Wolfspeed) a warning that the datacenter halo isn't actually profitable yet?
**Chatter:** high — earnings season plus Burry's broader semis short kept this group volatile.

### 8. Infra software: does AI expand the platform, or route around it?
**Names in play:** DDOG, SNOW, NET, MDB
**Bull side:**
- Datadog unveiled 100+ AI/security features at DASH with Q2 revenue +36%; Cloudflare's Q2 beat cited "agentic internet" traffic now exceeding human traffic on its network; MongoDB rallied 37% in a month on AI-native product launches (unified document+vector store for agent memory/RAG).
**Bear side:**
- Datadog stock fell 17% on its own earnings pop-and-drop on fears of tougher forward comps; ClickHouse's CEO publicly argued (on X) that AI agents prefer fast queries that get "very expensive" on Snowflake's architecture — a direct competitive bear thesis; a recurring "commodity Postgres wins" narrative argues AI-written code defaults to free, simple databases, undercutting MongoDB's paid complexity.
**The crux:** does AI multiply data/observability volume (bull case) or bypass legacy platforms for cheaper AI-native alternatives (bear case)?
**Chatter:** high — the "SaaSocalypse" framing recurs across multiple outlets this window.
