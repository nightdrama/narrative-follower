### 1. AI capex/demand durability vs. cycle-turn and supply-bullwhip risk
**Names in play:** NVDA, AMD, INTC, TSM, AMAT, LRCX, KLAC, ASML, MU, CBRS (spillover: DELL, HPE, ONTO, ENTG, CAMT)
**Bull side:**
- "Receipts, not forecasts": old A100s (5-6+ years old) still trading *above* original price; NVDA has locked TSMC 1.6nm/A16 capacity for post-Rubin "Feynman" (H2 2028), signaling multi-year visibility, not speculation (@TheRealBirnbaum debunking Burry's depreciation thesis).
- Hard numbers across the equipment/foundry chain: TSM Q2 revenue +36%, net income +77%, 2nm ramping; AMAT record quarter with 8-quarter backlog; LRCX now models $9-10B WFE per $100B AI capex (up from $8B); ASML raised FY26 guide to €43-45B with €38.9B backlog.
- AMD lifted its 2030 DC-AI accelerator TAM to $1.4T (from $1T); Baird raised AMD PT to $1,250 (Street-high) post "Advancing AI" event.
**Bear side:**
- Michael Burry's fresh NVDA/semis shorts and Steve Eisman's "Achilles heel" comments (lab concentration, cheaper Chinese models, price wars) anchor the skeptic case; memory/chip inventories (85%+ WIP) building in the "tens of billions" are framed as a bullwhip waiting to hit pricing.
- Capex/D&A intensity is at 2018-cycle peaks and margins are at all-time highs — the classic setup that historically invites new supply and margin compression (@InvestiBrew capital-cycle thread, cited across multiple sections).
- AMAT and other "beat and raised" names sold off anyway post-print, read by bears as valuation fatigue, not fundamentals.
**The crux:** is 2026-27 capex tracking real installed-base utilization and booked multi-year contracts, or is the whole chain building 2028 capacity against demand that hasn't shown up in cash flow yet?
**Chatter:** elevated — the single most cross-referenced debate this cycle, amplified by Burry's name recognition; NVDA earnings (Aug 26) flagged repeatedly as the next tone-setter.

### 2. Memory supercycle: structural AI re-rate vs. incoming oversupply
**Names in play:** MU, SNDK, SKHY, WDC, STX (spillover: VSH via MLCC tightness, AAPL sourcing)
**Bull side:**
- AI has structurally shifted memory demand (HBM/DRAM/NAND as the actual compute bottleneck) rather than a repeat PC/phone cycle; NAND shortage seen persisting into 2027 per Phison's CEO, HBM sold out through 2026-27.
- SanDisk's Aug 13 Investor Day guided mid-to-high-teens revenue growth through FY2028-30 with 100% of remaining FCF returned to shareholders; JPMorgan initiated SNDK Overweight on a "structural inflection" thesis.
- Explosive recent moves (SNDK +35%, STX +20%, WDC +17%, MU +11% in a week) and cheap multiples relative to the growth (MU ~6x forward) keep bulls buying dips.
- MLCC tightness is spilling into AI-server demand crowding out consumer/auto-grade parts (VSH benefiting), echoing the DRAM squeeze pattern.
**Bear side:**
- China's CXMT is scaling DRAM share and just raised ~$8.6B post-IPO to fund fabs that will close the technology gap over time — the classic "pricing invites supply" setup.
- Inventory/WIP math (bit-growth outrunning HBM mix) pointed to a 2027 glut in a widely-cited bear thread last weekend — largely faded (not refuted) after SanDisk's Investor Day.
- Burry reportedly still short MU/SNDK/SOXX, framed by bears as shorting the supercycle itself, not just a name.
**The crux:** does HBM/AI demand keep absorbing new bit supply faster than CXMT and incumbents add conventional capacity, or does the 2026 pricing spike simply pull forward the next glut?
**Chatter:** high — dozens of posts, several with 5k-35k+ views; sentiment flipped quickly from "cycle over" to renewed bullishness within the window.

### 3. Optics/photonics: sold-out pricing-power supercycle, almost no bear pushback
**Names in play:** LITE, COHR, AAOI, FN, ALAB (spillover: STM silicon photonics, CRDO, TSEM)
**Bull side:**
- Everything is capacity-gated, not demand-gated: LITE lasers sold out through 2028 despite active expansion; AAOI negotiating 3-year laser supply deals and reports a 4th qualified supplier plus >$200M order book; long-term agreements are locking in pricing and shifting capex risk to customers (LITE-AXTI $87.5M customer-funded deposits through 2031).
- Historically deflationary optics pricing is flipping — LITE reportedly able to raise CW laser prices on better yields, expanding gross margin above 50% non-GAAP.
- 1.6T ramp is running ahead of schedule (LITE: ">80% YoY laser output, incredibly fast"); CPO/NPO framed as additive TAM, not cannibalization, of pluggables — ALAB upgraded to Outperform (PT $350) on this basis after a Q2 beat (+104% YoY revenue).
- STM's silicon photonics (PIC100) getting smart-money buying (Druckenmiller/Duquesne added in Q2 13F) on a thesis that AI datacenter revenue guide (raised to $1B+ in 2026, $2B+ in 2027) is underpriced.
**Bear side:**
- Thin and mostly execution-risk rather than demand-risk: AAOI's mid-2027 targets require flawless capacity scaling (200k→650k+ units/month) with zero qualification slips; China is ramping optics capacity aggressively and could reroute through third countries.
- A few posts flag that napkin-math valuations on AAOI/COHR/CRDO "look insane" even assuming continued growth — a valuation-only objection, not a demand pushback.
**The crux:** this is currently a one-sided bull narrative — the real test is whether anyone starts contesting the demand numbers once the next earnings cycle hits, or whether valuation alone becomes the only bear argument left standing.
**Chatter:** moderate-to-high — the most one-directional (bullish) major theme in the portfolio, driven by fresh LITE/COHR/AAOI earnings and an ALAB upgrade.

### 4. AI power & electrification buildout: Jevons-paradox demand vs. efficiency/grid-bottleneck ceiling
**Names in play:** GEV, HWM, VRT, ETN, PWR, STRL (spillover: NVTS, STM as AI-power semis; nuclear/solar largely quiet)
**Bull side:**
- Jevons-paradox argument: cheaper/more efficient compute creates new use cases faster than it satisfies existing ones, so efficiency gains don't cap power demand — they expand it (echoed across DC-infrastructure and industrials threads).
- Hard numbers: GEV Q2 revenue +22% YoY, orders +88% to $24.2B, backlog $176B, guidance raised across the board; PWR backlog accelerating to $53.4B (from $48.5B) with guidance raised on every metric; VRT Q2 sales +24%, organic growth guidance lifted to 30-32%.
- New vendor validation: ETN-Trane joint AI-datacenter power architecture (up to 15% better efficiency, 80% less copper, aligned with Nvidia specs); NVIDIA formally added NVTS to its 800V DC power ecosystem.
**Bear side:**
- Efficiency counterargument: falling energy-per-AI-task (one estimate: 18x intelligence-per-joule improvement) could let hyperscalers pocket savings rather than reinvest, capping deployment growth even as workloads get more complex.
- Physical bottlenecks are structural, not cyclical: multi-year grid interconnection queues and labor/materials constraints could delay energization regardless of demand; HWM's "shortage moat" is challenged by reports SpaceX may enter turbine-blade manufacturing (though not meaningful for 4+ years).
- Valuation: GEV explicitly called "expensive" relative to HWM/ATI even by some bulls in the group; STRL trading at high multiples with technicals still in a downtrend.
**The crux:** does AI power demand keep outrunning grid/labor/equipment supply for years (the bull base case), or do efficiency gains plus interconnection delays cap the buildout before backlogs fully convert to revenue?
**Chatter:** moderate, concentrated on GEV/VRT/ETN/PWR earnings and the ETN-Trane news; most of DC Infrastructure's smaller names (FIX, IESC, EME, MTZ, POWL, ENS) saw almost no discussion, and nuclear/solar (OKLO, CEG, CCJ, FSLR, ENPH) were essentially silent this window.

### 7. CPU/x86 share war: AMD momentum vs. Intel turnaround priced ahead of numbers
**Names in play:** AMD, INTC (ARM, QCOM, DELL, HPE mostly silent)
**Bull side (AMD):**
- Chiplet architecture (Infinity Fabric, mixed nodes) is credited with the EPYC share gains now extending to MI450-series AI GPUs (300B+ transistors across 20+ chiplets); Dr. Su frames AI as a multi-year "supercycle" with CPU:GPU ratios shifting toward 1:1, making CPUs an additive AI beneficiary, not a legacy afterthought.
- DC revenue +16% QoQ; Baird's Street-high $1,250 PT followed the "Advancing AI" event and the raised 2030 TAM.
**Bull side (Intel):**
- DC revenue +23% QoQ (outpacing AMD's +16%); CEO Lip-Bu Tan bought $10M of shares; $19.7B capital raise funding the 14A node ramp, with the Ohio fab timeline reportedly pulled forward to 2028.
**Bear side:**
- Intel trades at a rich multiple relative to still-negative margins and lagging revenue growth versus NVDA/AMD, with the market pricing in a turnaround that hasn't shown up in shipped revenue yet; AMD, meanwhile, is capacity-constrained via its TSMC dependency (no owned fabs), unlike Intel's internal foundry ramp.
- Broader cycle bears note CPU TAM is structurally smaller than GPU/accelerator TAM and doesn't carry the same "residual value" argument bulls use for GPUs.
**The crux:** does Intel's fab-driven turnaround convert DC share gains into margin before AMD's chiplet/TSMC advantage compounds further, or is the market front-running an execution story on both sides that outpaces the actual numbers?
**Chatter:** high on AMD (several posts 10k-50k+ views), moderate on Intel; ARM/QCOM/DELL/HPE saw scattered technicals with no real debate.
