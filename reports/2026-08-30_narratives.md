## Top debates

### 1. AI capex: generational buildout vs. circular-financing bubble
**Names in play:** NVDA, MSFT, META, GOOGL, AMZN, ORCL, CRWV, NBIS, MU, PLTR, AVGO
**Bull side:**
- Nvidia's Aug 27 print: Q2 revenue $96.2B (>2x YoY, beat $92.2B consensus), data-center revenue +117% YoY to $89B, Q3 guide $108B vs. $104.2B consensus — the hyperscaler capex is converting into real, beat-and-raise revenue, not just promises.
- CoreWeave's ~$104B backlog and Oracle's RPO run to $638B (~9.5x trailing revenue) are cited as evidence contracted demand, not speculation, is driving the buildout; combined Big Tech (GOOGL/MSFT/AMZN/META) capex guided to ~$724B in 2026, ~$950B in 2027.
- Microsoft's July 29 print (+16% on the day) is held up as proof the spend is monetizing: Azure reacceleration, capex guidance not raised, resumed buybacks, ~$20B FCF after capex.
**Bear side:**
- Michael Burry has escalated a public circular-financing critique: Nvidia guaranteeing up to $125B (25%) of new "AI Compute Infrastructure Financing Platforms" with six banks, plus a $105B OpenAI guarantee and a $500B AI financing deal he calls "Enron-like" (Nvidia has publicly pushed back). He also disclosed a fresh Micron short and has a much-mocked $1 Palantir price target alongside a valuation critique many see as having real merit.
- Meta's July 29 print (-8% on the day) is the counter-example to Microsoft: beat revenue but missed EPS as AI infra costs ate the quarter, near-zero FCF after capex, buybacks halted three straight quarters, and — critically — Meta raised 2026 capex guidance while Microsoft didn't. Same sector, same week, opposite verdict, purely on capex-guidance direction and cash generation.
- CoreWeave's real economics are questioned: ~90% gross margin but net losses, debt exceeding equity, current ratio below 1; Oracle burned ~$23.7B of FY26 FCF against $55.7B capex and is leaning on new debt (~$40B more planned).
**The crux:** whether the AI-infra spend is now discriminating cleanly between capex spenders showing monetization (Microsoft, Amazon) and those still burning cash to build it (Meta, Oracle, CoreWeave) — and whether the financing structures underneath it (vendor guarantees, warrants, circular equity stakes) are prudent capital deployment or a leverage chain that snaps on the first demand air pocket.
**Chatter:** elevated — the largest, most persistent theme across every section, amplified by Nvidia's earnings this week.

### 2. Memory/HBM pricing supercycle — durable through 2027+, or a Burry-flagged top?
**Names in play:** MU, WDC, SNDK, STX, SKHY, SIMO, RMBS; spills into NVDA's own margin guide and CAMT/ONTO (HBM packaging)
**Bull side:**
- TrendForce: DRAM contract prices +93-98% QoQ in Q1'26, another +58-63% in Q2; NAND +85-90% then +55-60%. Micron's CEO says demand "continues to significantly exceed supply" through 2027+, with 16 multi-year customer agreements signed; SK hynix and Samsung reportedly sold out 2027 HBM capacity.
- Wall Street is overwhelmingly bullish: 40-analyst "Strong Buy" consensus on MU (UBS $1,625 PT); SanDisk laid out an 80% gross-margin, 50% adjusted-FCF-margin framework for FY28-30 with a $14B buyback (Bernstein PT $3,000).
**Bear side:**
- Michael Burry disclosed a fresh Micron short tied to broader AI-bubble/circular-financing and tech-obsolescence arguments, against a stock up >900% trailing twelve months.
- A mid-August WSJ report on pricing triggered a sharp complex-wide selloff (SanDisk -9%, Micron -7%, WDC -5% same day), and Citi expects pricing to peak around Q2 CY2027. WDC and SNDK both beat on revenue/EPS in their latest prints but guided below an inflated bar and still fell (WDC -14%, SNDK -8%) — strong fundamentals, punished stocks.
- Nvidia itself flagged rising memory input costs compressing its own gross-margin guide (71-72% vs. 75%) — a sign memory inflation is now biting the very companies that benefit from AI capex, and SK hynix approving $38B of new fab capacity is a bearish supply signal for pricing longevity.
**The crux:** whether hyperscaler HBM/DRAM commitments hold durably past 2027, or whether today's pricing is a temporary supply squeeze that both new capacity and Burry-style skepticism will unwind.
**Chatter:** elevated, with sharp day-to-day/week-to-week reversals — the most actively whipsawed section in the portfolio.

### 3. Custom silicon ASIC wars: Broadcom vs. Marvell vs. Nvidia

**Bull side (Marvell):** Google expanded its custom AI chip partnership with Marvell, including a warrant tied to up to $120B of future purchases; MRVL actually beat on its Aug 27 print (EPS $0.94, revenue +37% YoY to $2.74B) and raised full-year guidance.
**Bull side (Broadcom):** AVGO is reportedly exploring up to $100B in debt financing to back AI-chip deals for Anthropic and others, which bulls read as cementing its centrality to AI infrastructure.
**Bear side:** AVGO fell ~6.2% (~$87B market cap) on the Google-Marvell news, read as a direct hyperscaler diversification away from its dominant custom-silicon position; MRVL fell in sympathy (~5.6%) even on a beat-and-raise print, and again sold off after-hours despite good numbers — "investors wanted a much bigger beat." Nvidia's own PC-CPU entry is a fresh competitive threat layered on top, knocking INTC/AMD ~4.2% and pressuring QCOM/ARM (~-6%/-5%) the same week.
**The crux:** whether Google's expanded Marvell deal is a genuine, repeatable share shift in custom AI silicon away from Broadcom, or a single large contract being over-read — Broadcom's Sept 2 earnings is the next real test.
**Chatter:** elevated — the sharpest single-stock reactions (both directions) in the CPU/compute cluster this week.

### 5. AI power bottleneck: nuclear/SMR/hydrogen speculation vs. the "picks and shovels" buildout

**Bull side:**

Constellation raised FY26 EPS guidance on 920MW of new hyperscaler nuclear PPAs; Cameco has a DOE-backed $17.5B conditional loan for Westinghouse reactor components.
**Bear side:**
- The SMR/advanced-nuclear trade is being called an actively deflating bubble: NuScale's Q2 revenue was just $75,000 (vs. $8M a year earlier) alongside a new $750M dilutive share-sale program; Citi cut it to Sell ($6.50 PT); a trade-press piece cites short sellers up $2.1B on bets against the SMR group, noting most developers have no revenue and no reactors, with commercial deployment pushed to 2030+. Centrus (LEU) has fallen sharply off its highs on margin compression. In hydrogen, the trade is reportedly "narrowing to one name" (Bloom Energy, at 78x forward earnings) while Plug Power and FuelCell ride index-inclusion/risk-on sentiment with little underlying news.
- Political risk is now a live, named bear case for the whole DC-power-buildout trade: New York enacted a statewide moratorium on large data centers, and Bloomberg has flagged broader "political opposition to AI data centers" as a risk to revenue-recognition timing against equipment makers' backlogs.
**The crux:** whether nuclear/SMR/hydrogen names are pricing in power deals that will actually materialize on the stated timeline, or repeating a hype-then-violent-repricing pattern (LEU, Oklo, NuScale) — versus the steadier, backlog-driven DC-infrastructure equipment names, whose main risk is political/permitting delay rather than demand.
**Chatter:** elevated for nuclear/SMR/hydrogen tickers specifically; normal-to-elevated and steadier for the DC-infrastructure builders.

### 7. Foundry and export-control geopolitics
**Names in play:** TSM, LRCX, KLAC, ASML, ENTG, NXPI, INTC
**Bull side:** TSMC holds 73% pure-play foundry share with the segment growing 29% YoY, Arizona fab revenue +145% YoY, and a $29B 2026 capex authorization; Lam Research's CEO raised the 2026 global wafer-fab-equipment forecast to $150B; KLA's CEO says momentum is "accelerating" into 2027 on 70%+ advanced-packaging growth; Intel's turnaround narrative is being fed by Nvidia's ~$5B equity stake (now worth ~$30B) and a US government 10% equity stake.

**The crux:** whether China-exposure headline risk is a real, compounding earnings threat to TSM/LRCX/KLAC/ASML, or noise around an otherwise accelerating AI-driven capex cycle — and whether Intel's turnaround can reach real profitability before the capex bill and share losses catch up.
**Chatter:** normal, with periodic elevated spikes on specific headlines (export-control news, earnings).
